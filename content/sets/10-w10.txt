@ w10 | Week 10 | Session of 3 October, deck PSOSM_10 (headed "Week 11 Content Part 1") — "Beware of What You Share: Inferring Home Location in Social Networks" and VPN / HTTPS
N: The paper infers users' home city on Foursquare, Google+ and Twitter from public attributes and friends' locations, using simple majority voting. Headline accuracy: Foursquare ~67%, Google+ ~72%, Twitter ~82%.
N: Residence coordinates: ~60% within ~6 km for Foursquare and Twitter, only ~10% for Google+ (its education/employment/address don't track where people live now; address filled by 5,162 of 27M users).
N: Free-text locations were geocoded with the Yahoo! Places Finder API. Its "quality" (0–99) is a granularity level (40 = city), not a probability. Geotagged tweets are the only 100% clean attribute, but only ~0.5% of tweets have them.
N: Voting classes: Class 0 = one vote (taken as the answer) · Class 1 = several votes with a clear winner · Class 2 = a tie (can't be inferred; excluded from accuracy).
N: Foursquare single models: Mayorship 60.12% > Tip 59.11% > Like 55.89% > Friend 53.51%. Google+: Friend 50.89% > All 30.22% > Education 21.57% > Employment 7.64%.
N: VPN vs HTTPS: HTTPS encrypts browser ↔ website; a VPN encrypts device ↔ VPN server and hides your IP from sites. Neither hides your logged-in account, cookies or behaviour from the platform, and a VPN shifts trust from the ISP to the VPN provider.
