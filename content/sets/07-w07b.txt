N: Attack taxonomy: Physical (hardware/access) · Syntactic (code and protocol flaws) · Semantic (meaning: phishing, money mules, Nigerian scams). Phishing variants: spear (specific person), whaling (executives), vishing (voice), smishing (SMS), angler (fake customer-support accounts).
N: Nudge study (picture, timer, sentiment) measured privacy-setting changes, cancelled/edited posts, posting frequency and topic sensitivity. The timer interrupts System 1 so System 2 can reconsider.

Q: According to the graph-theoretic rationale for link farming, why does high in-degree benefit a node's PageRank?
A: In-degree directly multiplies the damping factor.
B: PageRank propagates authority through incoming links, so more (and better) inbound links increase the score recursively.
C: Search engines cap PageRank by out-degree.
D: In-degree sets crawl frequency, used as a proxy for PageRank.
=: B
?: Each inbound link is a vote weighted by the linker's importance; link farms harvest that. The damping factor is a global constant.

Q: Link farming is also referred to by which alternate terms in the slides?
A: Cloaking or keyword stuffing
B: Spamdexing or spamexing
C: Doorway pages or cloaked redirects
D: Sybil attacks or botnetting
=: B
?: Spam + indexing: manipulating the search index through artificial links.

Q: Why is "follower-rank"/in-degree especially consequential on Twitter compared with a generic web page?

D: Twitter in-degree is capped.

Q: Zhang & Paxson detected automated activity on Twitter using only data from the tweets themselves. Why is this methodologically notable?

C: It uses follower/following ratios from full graph crawls.
D: It uses IP geolocation from network access.

Q: What key structural difference distinguishes link farming on Twitter from link farming on the Web?
A: On Twitter only bots participate; on the Web only humans.
B: On Twitter, legitimate popular users also engage in reciprocal following, not just spammers, unlike the Web where it's almost exclusively spammers.
C: On the Web it is legal; on Twitter it is criminal.
D: On Twitter it reduces in-degree.

Q: The social etiquette of "following back" on Twitter mainly complicates spam detection because it:

Q: In the Thomas et al. "Suspended Accounts" study, what share of spam accounts were suspended within one day of their first tweet, and what does it imply?

Q: Only about 9% of spam accounts formed relationships with regular users, while 17% hijacked trends and 52% used unsolicited mentions. What is the dominant spam distribution vector?

Q: The "@spam: The Underground on 140 Characters or Less" paper reports a 0.13% clickthrough rate on Twitter spam, high compared with email spam. What is the most likely explanation?

Q: In the spam-targeting diagram (nodes A, B, S, C), S is the spammer. How do "spam-targets" and "spam-followers" differ?

Q: An analysis reports 82% overlap between spam-followers and spam-targets (1,134,379 of 1,383,214 combined). What does this most strongly suggest?
