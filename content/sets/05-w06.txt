@ w06 | Week 6 | Session of 29 August — centrality, e-crime and spam, fake news detection tools
N: Degree (in-degree = followers, out-degree = following) is the easiest measure to fake with bots. Betweenness marks bridges and brokers — low degree can still mean high betweenness. Closeness = short average distance. Eigenvector = connected to important nodes. A node can be high on several at once; none implies another.
N: E-crime: clickbait = sensational headlines for clicks · link farming = artificial hyperlink networks to game ranking · spamdexing = deceptive manipulation of search indexing (keyword stuffing, hidden text) · spam followers = fake accounts that follow someone · reciprocity analysis is a measurement, not an attack.
N: Tools: TweetCred (Twitter, browser plug-in, real-time score) → Facebook Inspector (Facebook) → WhatsFarzi (WhatsApp app). CredEye = web evidence for a claim. Birdwatch = crowd notes shown on diverse consensus; HawkEye tests its robustness.
N: Multimodal detectors: EANN (adversarial, event-invariant features) · MVAE (variational autoencoder, shared latent space) · SpotFake (BERT text + VGG image).

Q: An undirected network has edges A–B, A–C, B–D, C–D, D–E. Which node has the highest betweenness centrality?
A: A
B: B
C: D
D: E
=: C
?: E reaches A, B and C only through D, and D also sits on paths between B and C.

Q: In a directed network, A→B means A follows B. User X has 250 followers, follows 20 users, and lies on many shortest paths between weakly connected communities. Which statement is most accurate?
A: X necessarily has the highest out-degree centrality.
B: X necessarily has the highest in-degree centrality.
C: X may have high in-degree and betweenness, but relatively low out-degree.
D: X must have the highest closeness centrality.
=: C
?: 250 followers = high in-degree; following 20 = low out-degree; bridging communities = high betweenness.

Q: Node P has degree 20 but its neighbours form one tight community. Node Q has degree 6 but connects two otherwise disconnected communities. Which conclusion is most defensible?
A: P must have greater betweenness because its degree is larger.
B: Q can have greater betweenness despite having lower degree.
C: P and Q must have identical betweenness.
D: Degree and betweenness are mathematically equivalent.
=: B
?: Shortest paths between the two communities must pass through Q, not P.

Q: A node has very short average shortest-path distances to all other nodes in a connected network. What does this primarily indicate?
A: The node has the largest number of followers.
B: The node can reach other nodes efficiently through the network.
C: The node lies on every shortest path.
D: The node has the highest eigenvector centrality.
=: B
?: That is high closeness centrality — good for fast dissemination.

Q: Two users have the same number of connections. User A is connected mostly to low-degree peripheral users; User B to several highly influential users. Who has higher eigenvector centrality?
A: User A
B: User B
C: Both must be equal
D: Neither can have eigenvector centrality
=: B
?: Eigenvector centrality weights each link by the importance of the neighbour.

Q: In a Twitter-like network (A→B means A follows B), user X follows 500 accounts but is followed by only 10. Which statement is correct?
A: X has high in-degree and low out-degree.
B: X has low in-degree and high out-degree.
C: X has high betweenness automatically.
D: X necessarily has high eigenvector centrality.
=: B
?: Following = outgoing edges (500); followers = incoming edges (10).

Q: Which scenario is the strongest example of clickbait rather than search-engine spam?

Q: Many fake accounts all connect to one central account, and the fakes have no other meaningful links. Which centrality is most directly inflated?
A: Degree centrality
B: Betweenness centrality necessarily

Q: Node X has moderate degree, connects two otherwise separated communities, is only a few steps from most nodes, and links to several highly influential users. Which measures could all be high for X?
A: Degree only
B: Betweenness only
C: Betweenness, closeness, and eigenvector centrality

=: C
