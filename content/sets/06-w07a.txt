N: High out-degree + low in-degree = broadcaster or possible spammer. PageRank weighs who links to you, not only how many.
N: Policing study, citizen-police threads (CP&C) vs citizen-only (CC): comments 3.34 vs 3.69 (9.49% lower), likes 9.4 vs 13.38 (29.75% lower), negative affect 0.021 vs 0.018 (16.67% higher in CP&C), anxiety 0.001 vs 0.003 (200% higher in CC), arousal 4.4 vs 3.9 (12.82% higher in CP&C), "I" 0.008 vs 0.014. Valence and arousal are emotional measures; interpersonal focus, social orientation and cognition are social/cognitive (LIWC).
N: Faking Sandy (2012): 10,350 unique fake-image tweets; 86% were retweets; top 30 users (0.3%) caused 90% of retweets; follower links explained only 11% of spread; Decision Tree 97%, tweet features beat user features.

Q: In a directed network, an account has very high out-degree but very low in-degree. Which interpretation is most appropriate?
A: The account is necessarily the most influential node.
B: The account broadcasts information widely but receives relatively little attention from other nodes.
C: The account must have the highest betweenness.
D: The account must have the highest eigenvector centrality.
=: B
?: This fits a broadcaster, promoter or spam account; it implies nothing about betweenness or eigenvector centrality.

Q: User A has 100 followers, mostly ordinary users. User B has only 30 followers, but they are highly influential accounts. Which user can have higher eigenvector centrality?
A: A, because 100 is greater than 30.
B: B, because eigenvector centrality considers the importance of connected neighbors.
C: A, because eigenvector centrality is identical to degree centrality.
D: Neither, because it only applies to undirected networks.
=: B
?: Eigenvector centrality measures the quality of connections, not just their number.

Q: Two dense communities are connected by a single user X with four connections. Another user has 150 connections entirely inside one community. Which statement is most defensible?
A: The second user must have higher betweenness because degree is higher.
B: X may have substantially higher betweenness because many shortest paths between communities pass through X.
C: X must have higher eigenvector centrality.
D: Betweenness depends only on the number of followers.
=: B
?: Removing X could disconnect the two communities; the 150-link user is only central locally.

Q: In a connected network, user P has the smallest average shortest-path distance to all other users. Which conclusion follows most directly?
A: P necessarily has the highest degree.
B: P necessarily has the highest betweenness.
C: P has high closeness centrality.
D: P necessarily has the highest eigenvector centrality.
=: C
?: Minimum distance to everyone is the definition of closeness.

Q: A node has the highest degree, betweenness and closeness centrality in a network. Which statement is logically valid?
A: It must also have the highest eigenvector centrality.
B: It must have the lowest PageRank.
C: It is structurally important according to several different notions of centrality.
D: It must be a spammer.
=: C
?: Eigenvector also depends on neighbours' importance, and legitimate hubs can be highly central.

Q: Two web pages each have 100 incoming links. Page A's come from 100 obscure pages; Page B's from five extremely authoritative pages. Which statement is most plausible?

B: Page B can have higher PageRank because PageRank incorporates the importance of linking pages.

Q: A group of websites creates thousands of reciprocal hyperlinks mainly to manipulate search rankings without providing real information. Which phenomenon is this?

Q: Suspected link farmers have unusually high in-degree compared with random users. Which interpretation is strongest?
